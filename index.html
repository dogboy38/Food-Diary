The rules that do not change

Lunch and dinner must be chewed. Solid food. No shake, no bar, no exception.

Protein bars: zero per day. Whey: maximum 2 scoops, both stirred into food. The only permitted liquid protein is the 3–6 pm rescue — ½ cup Greek yogurt + 1 scoop whey — once daily, and only when the alternative is cookies.

Out of time? Substitute a different solid, never a shake. Two konbini chicken packs = 46 g protein for 220 kcal and covers any missed meal.

Free at any meal, uncounted: salad greens with ponzu, clear soup, any non-starchy vegetable, black coffee, tea, water, 0.00% beer. Eat the vegetables first — order matters for satiety.

Never: fried · deep-fried · breaded · battered · tempura · karaage · katsu · cream or cheese sauce · teriyaki or any sweet glaze.

Chicken breast is 250 kcal grilled and over 700 as karaage. The preparation matters more than the ingredient.

One Oikos pot is 113 g — 12 g protein, 70 kcal. Highest protein per calorie in the case. As a snack one pot is right; as a meal protein you need two or three, so use the stepper rather than logging it once.

Large plain tubs are cheaper per gram if you can find them, and single-serve pots skew heavily toward the flavoured versions — check every label.

Plain only. A flavoured cup carries up to 18 g of sugar, most of a day's budget, and feeds exactly the sweet-craving loop. Add berries or cinnamon yourself and you land at 6 g instead.

Why it suits you specifically: dairy protein lowers uric acid (yours is 8.0), unlike meat and seafood purines, and it doesn't work against LDL the way hard cheese does. But at 10–12 g per 100 g it is a filler, not an anchor — three cups to match one chicken breast.

Snacks — what counts and what doesn't

Smoothies are fine, with conditions. Whey + whole fruit + cottage cheese or plain Greek yogurt, blended, no added sugar and no juice as the base. Whole fruit keeps the fibre intact and the thickness forces you to drink it slowly — that is most of why it still counts as food rather than as the liquid-protein loophole. It stands in for the 3–6 pm rescue, not for a chewed lunch or dinner.

Not protein snacks: nuts are a fat snack (almonds are 6 g protein per 175 kcal) — keep to a measured 3 tbsp with fruit. Cheese other than cottage: ~6 g per 100 kcal, high saturated fat against LDL 149. Protein bars: zero, always. Jerky and dried squid: sodium, against BP 133/84. Dried mango is candy, not fruit.

Protein bars stay at zero — including the good ones. MOSH High Protein is nutritionally sound (20 g protein, 6 g fibre, 3 g sugar) but carries creatine, which raises serum creatinine and would blind the eGFR you are watching, and ashwagandha, which has case reports of liver injury against a γ-GTP of 119. One in an airport is not a problem; a daily habit is.

Salami swap: bresaola 10 g / 50 kcal, prosciutto 8 g / 65. Both beat salami (6 g / 100). Cap at 30–50 g for sodium.

Beef: lean cuts only, 150 g, two to three times a week maximum — red meat is high-purine against uric acid 8.0, and marbled cuts work against LDL. A5 wagyu is 11 g protein per 500 kcal: an occasion, not a protein source.

Shrimp is fine. Dietary cholesterol is not what drives blood cholesterol — saturated fat is, and shrimp has 0.4 g per 200 g. It also raises HDL, which at 36 you need.

Non-alcoholic beer: 0.00% only, 15–35 kcal. Avoid the 0.5% category. One with dinner — and if it makes you think about the real thing, stop and don't restock.

Why each element is there

Barley rice (mochimugi). Same cooking, same bowl — mix 30% into the rice cooker. Adds 6–8 g fibre and beta-glucan a day.

Oats + chia + barley = 3 g+ beta-glucan. At this dose viscous fibre lowers LDL 5–10%, and it is additive on top of a statin.

No fruit juice, ever. Liquid fructose without fibre drives liver fat and raises uric acid directly. Whole fruit is fine — the fibre changes the metabolism entirely.

Fibre before protein for satiety. 30 g a day slows gastric emptying and blunts the 3–6 pm crash. This is the mechanism that has been missing, not willpower.

Dairy at breakfast. Dairy protein lowers uric acid, unlike meat and seafood purines.

Weekly shop

Staples: rolled oats, chia seeds, mochimugi barley (Hakubaku 50 g packets), whey protein, plain Oikos or skyr pots (113 g, never flavoured) plus a large plain Greek tub for the oat bowl, frozen berries.

Freezer: cooked shrimp, edamame, green beans, broccoli, spinach. Frozen matches fresh on fibre and needs no washing or chopping.

Pantry: pouch lentils, canned black beans, canned chickpeas.

Fresh: cod or sea bass ×2, chicken breast, lean beef 150 g, eggs, tofu, bagged salad, shredded cabbage, carrots, avocado, apples ×3, kiwi ×4.

Konbini standing order: chicken packs ×8 — keep four at the office. That is what makes the no-shake rule survivable.

Condiments: ponzu (not sesame dressing — 70 kcal/tbsp against 10), olive oil, rice vinegar, lemon.

Ask the doctor — four items

1. CPAP re-titration and compliance data (AHI, hours used, leak rate). Apnea since 2007; RBC 567 and haematocrit 48.5 suggest it isn't controlled, and pressure requirements rise with weight. Undertreated apnea raises ghrelin and lowers leptin — the appetite is partly physiological. Highest-leverage item on the list.

2. Morning testosterone panel — total, free, SHBG, LH, drawn before 10 am. Never measured in four years of physicals. A gap to fill, not a known low.

3. Statin dose review. LDL 149 on therapy with documented coronary calcification (Agatston 12.9); target should be under 100. Also flag CK 346 in 2025 — statin muscle symptoms produce exactly the "used to train, now can't face it" experience.

4. Omega-3. With no salmon and no mackerel there's no dietary source left. Triglycerides 144, HDL 36 and fatty liver all argue for supplementation.

Also mention: eGFR drifting 69 → 74 → 67 → 63 with creatinine 0.98 at the top of range — 120 g protein daily is fine for normal kidney function but worth a sentence. And Lexapro 5 mg: emotional blunting and carbohydrate craving are recognised effects.

Why 13% matters

76.2 → 66.3 kg is a 13% loss, and the liver evidence is dose-graded: 5% or more reduces liver fat, 7% or more improves inflammation, 10% or more stabilises or reverses fibrosis.

You clear all three thresholds. γ-GTP 119 and visceral fat 147.9 cm² are the readouts that should move first — expect change by week 8.

Not medical advice — review with your physician. Ramp fibre over two weeks to avoid bloating, and take viscous fibre 1–2 hours apart from medication.
